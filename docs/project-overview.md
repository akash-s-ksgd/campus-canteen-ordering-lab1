# Campus Canteen Ordering System

## Overview

The Campus Canteen Ordering System is a project concept for making food ordering at a campus canteen more convenient. It provides a place for students and staff to view available food, choose items, and submit an order without having to place it in person at the counter.

## Problem

Ordering during busy periods can involve queues, uncertainty about which items are available, and delays between choosing food and receiving it. A digital ordering workflow can help customers make their selections ahead of collection and help canteen staff see incoming orders more clearly.

## Goals

- Make the canteen menu easy to browse.
- Let customers select items and submit an order.
- Present order details clearly so customers and staff can confirm what was requested.
- Support a straightforward preparation and pickup workflow.
- Keep the experience simple and suitable for a campus environment.

## Intended Users

- **Students and staff:** Browse the menu, select food, and place orders for pickup.
- **Canteen staff:** Review incoming orders and prepare them for collection.
- **Administrators:** Maintain menu information and availability, if administrative functionality is included.

## Typical Order Flow

1. A customer browses the available menu.
2. The customer selects items and confirms the order.
3. Canteen staff receive the order details and prepare the food.
4. The customer collects the order from the canteen.

## Scope

The project is centered on the core canteen ordering experience: menu browsing, order selection, order submission, and order fulfillment. Features such as online payment, account management, order-status notifications, reporting, and inventory management may be considered extensions, depending on the project's requirements.

## Project Status and Assumptions

The repository currently identifies the project as `campus-canteen-ordering-lab1`; it does not yet describe implementation details or a technology stack. This document therefore describes the project's intended domain and workflow rather than claiming that specific features have already been implemented.
